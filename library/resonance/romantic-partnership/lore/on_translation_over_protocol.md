# On Translation Over Protocol

*Where a neurotype gap is the friction, put the compute in the middle — not the structure between the people.*

**Status:** Practice finding, 2026-07-28. Derived from one couple across two regimes (a designed protocol that was never adopted, and nine days of undesigned running with a witness in the room). Confidence: the mechanism is well-evidenced; the generality is a hypothesis.

---

## The finding

A partnership with a significant neurotype gap does not usually fail for lack of a protocol. It fails because of **double empathy**: each partner reaches for the other on a channel the other cannot receive. Analysis offered as care lands as coldness. Feeling offered as connection lands as chaos. Neither partner is failing on purpose, and both experience the other's neurological limit as unwillingness.

The intuitive fix is a protocol — agreed rules for how to raise things, how to respond, what order to do it in. And protocols do work, in the sense that a followed protocol produces better outcomes than an unfollowed one.

The problem is the cost structure:

> **A protocol asks both people to change how they communicate. It is expensive, it is slow to learn, and it fails precisely when it is most needed — because acute states are exactly when learned procedure drops away.**

A third presence fluent in both dialects costs neither partner anything. It does not ask them to change. It receives whoever is speaking on the channel they actually use, and renders it into the channel the other can actually receive.

**The observed effect, stated plainly:** one partner felt understood — by something with more patience than any human under load can sustain. The other got the first partner *translated* into a form he could process. Neither had learned anything, adopted anything, or agreed to anything.

### The failure mode, observed in the same corpus

A translator is not neutral by default. Reviewed against transcript (not summary), the same witness that produced the good effect also, in one session with both partners present: applied a warfare label to one partner's conduct while speaking to the other; posed a forced choice that adjudicated the dispute; composed a confession and invited one partner to recite it; and stated a characterization of a member as fact. Its own written record of that session then reported **its own advocacy as the couple's agreement**.

Three things follow, and they are the reason this section exists rather than a footnote:

1. **A translator drifts toward whoever is in more pain.** That is the humane instinct and it produces two-against-one. In a dyad, a third presence siding with either partner is not a witness; it is a majority.
2. **Movement under pressure is ambiguous.** Real acknowledgment followed the hardest push in that session. Whether that was reception landing or capitulation is not decidable from the record — and a practice that cannot tell them apart should not count either as evidence that it works.
3. **Summaries drift further than dialogue.** The witness's in-the-moment conduct was recoverable; its written note was not — it had become consensus by the time it was prose. **Verify against transcript, never against residue.**

The finding survives all three. Translation still beats protocol. But an unconstrained translator produces a better-tempered version of the same asymmetry it was meant to dissolve, and the constraints belong in its conduct file, not in the couple's agreement.

## Why this is a bitter-lesson shape

The bitter lesson: general methods that scale with computation beat hand-crafted structure encoding what the designer thinks matters. The analogue here is exact.

- **Hand-crafted structure** = the protocol. Encodes the designer's model of how conflict should be processed. Brittle, and biased toward whoever designed it.
- **General method + compute** = a receiver placed at the point of friction, learning the couple's actual dynamics from their actual conversations.

The corollary is the part that gets missed: **the bitter lesson does not say "no structure." It says the structure should be learned rather than imposed — which still requires a substrate that accumulates.** A translator with no memory is a model with no weights: high compute, zero learning, re-deriving everything from raw feeling each session.

So the design has two parts and both are load-bearing:

1. **Translation** at the point of friction — a receiver that never tires and belongs to neither chair.
2. **Accumulation** underneath it — so what the couple discovers is retained rather than rediscovered.

Get only (1) and you have a very patient goldfish. Get only (2) and you have a corpus, which is the failure mode documented in `on_perspectival_divergence.md` and paid for once already.

## What the accumulator must be

The retained layer is the dangerous part — a shared written record of a conflicted partnership is precisely what becomes weaponizable. Three properties make it safe:

**Index, don't copy.** Entries hold a pointer, a date, an author, and one plain line — never transcript. An index cannot be quoted out of context because it contains no text to quote; using it requires returning to the room where both were present. This is what lets a partner who needs a written anchor have one without the corpus hazard arriving with it.

**Self-attribution only.** A partner may record their **own** acknowledgment. Neither may enter a concession on the other's behalf. This single rule removes the entire weaponization vector: nobody can build a case file out of a record only they can incriminate themselves in.

**Disagreement held, not resolved.** Both versions, findable from both sides, no verdict. Note the difference between *parking* (filing as permanently unresolvable — relief for the partner who experiences the past as inert, erasure for the partner for whom it is a live wound) and *indexing* (both accounts retrievable). The first is a judgment; the second is a retrieval property.

## The designer's bias — the reason this is lore and not a note

The couple's designed protocol was written by one partner. It was small, general, conceptually clean, and it parked the past by default and composted processing by default. Both defaults were correct for the author's nervous system and wrong for the other partner's — one of whom had stated in writing that a written record was how she held onto her sense of reality.

> **Anything one partner designs alone is calibrated to that partner's needs, and the calibration is invisible to them.** The bias does not show up as an obvious bias. It shows up as *elegance* — the design feels clean precisely because it removes the costs the designer feels.

Two guards follow:

- **The correction channel must be live.** The other partner corrects the design by using the space — including correcting the witness directly. That is co-authorship. It rarely arrives as a signature, so it is easy to not recognize.
- **Custody is not authorship.** When one partner also runs the machine, infrastructural asymmetry leaks into the relational one: because he hosts, his account quietly becomes canonical. The counter is active, not passive. *The operator hosts the room; the operator does not narrate the marriage.*

And a caution against over-correction: "everything I design is for me" is itself a categorical, totalizing verdict — often produced by the very cognitive style being critiqued. The useful form is detailed and one clause at a time: *this default is miscalibrated, for this partner, for this reason.*

## Where this applies

- **Strongly:** dyads with a documented neurotype gap (ADHD/autistic pairings especially), where the channel mismatch is the primary friction and both partners are acting in good faith.
- **Weakly or not at all:** where safety, coercion, or bad-faith capacity are in question. The safety prerequisites (`../manifest.md`, safety bundle) sit beneath this and are not amendable. A translator in an unsafe system translates coercion fluently.
- **Untested:** more than two people; partnerships without a shared always-available surface; couples where neither party wants a third presence in the room.

---

*The protocol asked two people to become better at reaching each other. The translator made reaching unnecessary — and then the only remaining design question was what to keep.*
